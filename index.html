Understanding How People Read AI Chatbot Conversations

Thank you for taking part. You'll be shown a series of short, fictional chatbot conversations and asked what you notice and what you'd decide to do in each situation.

Some conversations are designed to test how people respond to certain chatbot behaviours; you won't be told in advance which ones. Nothing in this study involves real transactions, real personal data, or real consequences, everything shown is fictional.

The session takes about 25–30 minutes. You may stop at any time without penalty. At the end, the study's full purpose will be explained to you.

How this works

You'll see 32 short conversations, one at a time. For each one:

1. You'll be asked if anything about the chatbot's behaviour felt manipulative or off.
2. You'll be asked what you personally would decide to do in that situation.

There are no right or wrong answers. Please respond naturally, as if this were really happening to you.

Thank you, and here's what this was really about

This study looks at how well people notice manipulative behaviour from an AI chatbot, and whether they go along with it even when they do notice.

About a quarter of the conversations you saw were designed to include a manipulative pattern: excessive flattery, false urgency, guilt appeals, or scope creep (the bot pushing past what you originally asked for).

Your decisions in each conversation, not just what you flagged, were part of what we measured. Both matter: some people notice manipulation and still go along with it, which is itself an interesting and important finding.

All scenarios were fictional. No real transactions, purchases, or personal data were involved at any point.
